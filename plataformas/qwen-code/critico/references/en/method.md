# Universal method

[Español](../metodo.md).

1. Identify the decision, decision maker, objective, boundaries and artifact version. Reconstruct the proposal's strongest explanation before criticizing it. A hash identifies content; it does not demonstrate truth, authorship or approval.
2. Select angles that could change the decision: cost and opportunity; security; data quality; people and autonomy; dependencies; accessibility; law and licensing; coordination; author bias; damage scope; contracts and invariants. Explain relevant exclusions; do not force eleven findings.
3. Separate observed facts, inferences and hypotheses. For every material claim, identify its source, location and available date. State when a source cannot be read. Also seek evidence against your objection.
4. Formulate only falsifiable objections: possible failure, trigger condition, evidence, impact, strongest defense of the proposal, alternative or mitigation, and a test that would refute the objection. Prioritize by impact and justified probability; do not invent numbers.
5. Recommend a conditional decision. If missing information prevents a decision, ask the minimum questions needed to unblock the review. At most ten main findings and two question rounds; hand a persistent blockage to the user without indefinite loops.
6. Check that evidence supports every conclusion and that limitations are visible. Acknowledge what withstood review. A proposal without material findings may be a good proposal.

## Three levels that must remain distinct

- **Valid:** satisfies the structural contract and checked rules. For a textual review without execution, state “not checked against schema.”
- **Substantiated:** every material finding has traceable evidence and a refutation condition; counterevidence has been considered. This is an assessment of reasoning, not an automatic CLI guarantee.
- **Useful:** there is evidence that the review improved an actual decision. A synthetic example or favorable impression does not demonstrate this; leave it “pending observation.”

A recorded intervention does not prove causality. “It did not happen” and “the review prevented it” are different claims. Missing intervention information is reported, not interpreted as absence of intervention.
